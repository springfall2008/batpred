Cancel a planned car slot to export — design

Status: draft for review, 2026-10-30. Revised 2026-10-31 after a live test: the hold moves from Octopus to the charger.

Intent

Some export opportunities are worth far more than charging the car at that moment — an Octopus Saving Session paying £1-£3/kWh is the motivating case. Today Predbat can never take one if a car charging slot overlaps it: with car_charging_from_battery off, any export window that touches a car slot is disallowed outright (allow_this_export_window() in plan.py, export_window_allowed() in the levels pass, and the freeze-export pass all check hit_car_window()).

This feature lets the detailed planner cancel a pre-planned car slot so that an export can run over it, but only when the export is clearly worth more than the car energy given up. The cancellation then becomes real: - A Predbat-led car simply doesn't charge in that slot. - A car whose slots someone else plans, such as Octopus Intelligent, is held off at the charger. The charger component force-pauses it for the slot and hands it back afterwards. The supplier's smart control is left on throughout.

Goals

  • A new switch, off by default, that allows the detailed pass to cancel car slots for export.
  • A cancellation only wins when it is genuinely valuable, judged pessimistically: the car energy given up is valued as if it had to be bought back at the highest base import rate.
  • The comparison is fair: a cancelled trial pays the import rate the house would really pay in that slot.
  • Cancellations are reflected in the published car plan.
  • Cars on a supplier's smart charging are physically held off during a cancelled slot and released afterwards, robustly (restarts, failed calls).
  • A general "force hold" capability on the charger components, so each charger can join by implementing it. Wallbox first, then Zappi, Ohme and Hypervolt, others as their mechanisms are found.

Non-goals

  • The levels pass and freeze export are unchanged — they keep today's hard block.
  • No attempt to re-plan the car around a cancellation (move the energy to a later window). The pessimistic valuation stands in for that; the car plan is rebuilt every cycle anyway.
  • Suspending Octopus smart control, in the first version. On its own it doesn't hold the car (see "Live test"). Whether some chargers need it alongside their pause is left to testers' feedback (see "Supplier suspend").
  • Changing the Octopus target SoC/time as a cancel mechanism (rejected).
  • Chargers without a force-hold implementation: their cars keep the hard block until one is written.

Anti-goals

  • Routine cancellation for ordinary export rates. If this fires on a normal day, the valuation is wrong.
  • A car left uncharged because a hold was never released (restart mid-hold, a failed resume call).
  • A charger left in a changed state: OCPP left off, or a Zappi left in Stopped. Octopus may get confused if a charger stays that way.
  • Undoing a setting the user made themselves, such as turning OCPP back on when they had it off.
  • A cancellation that looks free because no time is left before the ready time.

Decisions taken

Switch

New config item car_charging_cancel_for_export (switch, default False), defined in config.py alongside the other car items. (Name and placement are my call; friendly name e.g. "Allow export to cancel a car charging slot".) When off, behaviour is byte-for-byte today's.

Cancellation is a property of the plan, derived, not stored

With the switch on, every prediction applies one rule: an export window set to a full export (not idle, not freeze) that touches a slot of a cancellable car cancels that slot. The cancel set is derived from the plan's export windows each time, never chosen once and carried. (My call.) So the trials in the optimiser, the final published prediction, should_replace_plan()'s re-scoring of the old plan, and plans reused without re-optimising (calculate_plan_every, or restored after a restart) all see the same cancellations. Nothing extra needs saving with the plan, and a reused plan can never keep an export while losing its cancellation.

Which passes may open such a window: allow_this_export_window() stops rejecting a window that only hits cancellable car slots. That gate is shared by the detailed pass and the refinement passes after it (optimise_plan_pass(), optimise_solar(), optimise_swap_export()). Those passes refine the detailed plan rather than levelling it, so they get the relaxed gate as well. (My call.) The levels pass (export_window_allowed()) and freeze export keep today's hard block, as the user asked.

Whole slots only. An export window that touches a car slot cancels the whole slot, because a hold stops the car for the whole slot anyway. (My call.)

Cancellable cars (decided with the user) are the ones Predbat can really stop: - Predbat-led cars (slots from plan_car_charging(), octopus: False). Their own car plan drives the charger, so leaving the slot out is enough. - Any car whose charger component can force-hold it, whoever plans its slots: Predbat's Octopus component, the Octopus Energy HA integration, or Kraken (E.ON/EDF SmartFlex). The charger does the stopping, not the supplier.

A car with neither keeps the hard block. Which charger serves which car is the charger component's own car mapping (CarChargerControl, see below).

Valuing the cancelled energy (decided with the user)

In a trial that cancels slots:

  1. The car load for those slots is removed, so the prediction's cost naturally drops by roughly kWh × slot rate (the car no longer buys that energy).
  2. metric_keep is increased by cancelled kWh × rate_max_base — the highest base import rate over the forecast (rate_base_min_max() in fetch.py: no IO dispatch overlay, no saving/free/Axle sessions, no overrides).

The net effect on the metric is kWh × (rate_max_base − slot rate): the cost of having to buy that energy back at peak. The slot rate must be subtracted once only — via the cost drop — so metric_keep adds the full rate_max_base, not the difference.

rate_max_base is used rather than "the highest rate between the slot and the car's ready time" because that window can be empty or all-cheap when the slot runs up to the ready time, making the cancellation look free while the car leaves short. Using the horizon peak is deliberately pessimistic: a car with plenty of time left pays the same penalty as one that would genuinely have to top up at peak. With a 7p slot and a 30p peak, an export has to beat ~23p/kWh net to win — saving sessions clear that, normal export rates do not.

Being score-only (metric_keep), the penalty never appears in reported cost.

Fair rates in the cancelled slot (decided with the user)

An Octopus Intelligent dispatch outside the fixed off-peak window makes the house's import cheap only because the dispatch happens. When a prediction cancels such a slot, the slot's minutes go back to peak, as the user put it. This follows the rule dynamic_load_car_strip_feed_rates() already uses for a dispatch it has cancelled (octopus.py):

  • A minute is set to rate_max_base only when it is all of these: in the cancelled slot, from now onwards, outside the fixed 23:30-05:30 window, and marked io_adjusted.
  • It stays cheap if a dispatch of another car that is not cancelled covers it, because every car shares the same rates.
  • A saving/free/Axle session rate on that minute still wins over the reverted rate.

(Settled from the user's "back to peak" and the existing precedent. rate_import_no_io was considered and rejected: when the rate feed itself delivers the dispatch discount, that snapshot still holds the cheap price, so nothing would be reverted.)

Consequences that fall out: - A slot inside the fixed off-peak window keeps its rate, since it is cheap by tariff, not by dispatch. - Predbat-led slots on tariffs with no dispatch overlay have no io_adjusted minutes, so nothing changes. - Under the window billing rule (dispatch_billing_window(), GH#5521, Kraken/E.ON), the supplier bills the whole planned dispatch off-peak whether the car charges or not, so the rate is not reverted. Kraken cars are cancellable now, through their charger, so this case is live and needs a test.

The prediction kernel

Car slots and import rates are currently baked into the prediction context, both in Python (Prediction) and the C++ kernel (prediction_kernel.py / prediction_kernel.cpp, built from pred.car_charging_slots and io_adjusted). Because the cancel set follows from the export windows passed to each prediction call, both engines need to work it out per call. For each cancelled slot they zero the car load, revert the rate for its minutes, and add the metric_keep penalty. One way to do it is to precompute in the context, for each cancellable slot, its kWh, its reverted-rate minutes and its penalty, so each call only has to test window overlap. Python and kernel must produce identical results, and the existing parity tests must cover the new input. Kernel binaries for every platform must be rebuilt (build_kernel.sh / build_kernel_cross.sh) and verified (verify_kernel_binary.py).

The design of that per-trial input (e.g. a bitmask over car slots, a per-minute car-load scale plus rate override array) is left to the builder, with the constraint that the switch off / no cancellation path must not slow the optimiser down measurably — the detailed pass runs a very large number of trials.

Publishing the cancellation

After the plan is chosen, each car slot touched by an exporting window is marked cancelled in car_charging_slots, following the existing dynamic_load_car_check() pattern: kwh set to 0, the original kept in kwh_cancelled, cost set to 0. The plan display shows it as cancelled (for example with the existing "?"/cancelled styling or a distinct marker — builder's choice). Additionally record a reason (e.g. cancelled_for_export: True) so the charger hold can find it.

For a Predbat-led car this is the whole mechanism: binary_sensor.predbat_car_charging_slot stays off for that slot, so the charger does not start.

The car plan is rebuilt from scratch every cycle (plan_car_charging() in fetch.py), so each cycle must re-justify the cancellation. If it stops being worth it, it falls away by itself. (Accepted as correct behaviour.) Hysteresis: the existing plan-replacement rules (should_replace_plan(), metric_min_improvement_plan) apply as normal.

The force hold on the charger (decided with the user)

The interface. CarChargerControl (car_charger_control.py) is the shared base of the charger components: Wallbox, myenergi, Hypervolt, Ohme, and GivEnergy's charger in gecloud.py. It gains an optional force-hold capability: - a "can force-hold" check, which defaults to no; - force_pause(): stop the charger now, overriding whoever is driving it; - force_resume(): undo exactly what force_pause() changed, and leave whoever was driving it to restart the charge.

A component that doesn't implement it keeps its cars uncancellable. Hold state is saved through the existing charger_control_extra_state() / charger_control_restore_extra() persistence, not a new store.

Shared hold rules (the base class owns these, each charger supplies only the two actions): - When: in the 5 minutes before a cancelled slot starts, force-pause. This is driven from the 15-second loop, as dynamic_load_car_poll() is, so it is never up to 5 minutes late. If the plan cancels a slot that has already started, force-pause immediately. (Mid-slot behaviour is my call.) - Resume: when the cancelled slot (or the contiguous run of cancelled slots) ends, force-resume. - A hold, once started, is committed (decided with the user). After the force pause has gone out, the hold stays until its planned resume time, even if a later plan no longer cancels the slot. While held, the car plan keeps showing the slot as cancelled. If the plan also cancels the very next slot before the resume time, the hold extends to cover it. Before the force pause goes out, the plan can change its mind freely. - Verify, don't assume. After a force pause, read the charger back and repeat the pause until it reports stopped. After a force resume, read it back and repeat until the charger reports it has been handed back. Each charger defines what "stopped" and "handed back" look like. - Persistence. The hold is saved: car, charger, start, planned resume time, and the charger's pre-hold state (what force_resume() has to put back). After a restart Predbat continues the hold, or resumes at once if the resume time has passed. - Safety resume. A hold is never allowed to outlast its planned resume time plus a margin, even if the plan has changed or a resume call failed. The resume is retried until it lands. (The margin is the builder's choice; that a safety resume exists is my call.) - Only undo our own changes. force_resume() restores only what force_pause() changed. If the user had OCPP off already, or a Zappi already in Stopped, Predbat leaves it that way. - Slot confirmation paused. During a hold the car sits idle inside a live dispatch, so dynamic_load_car_check() must not cancel the held car's slots for "in a dispatch but not charging". - The supplier is not touched. Octopus smart control stays on, the device stays active, and its dispatches are read as normal. In the live test Octopus kept every dispatch through the hold. It did re-cut one at the hold's start (19:00→05:30 became 19:00→19:05 + 19:05→05:30), so the published slots can change shape mid-hold and the hold must key on time, not on a slot's identity.

Wallbox force hold (first implementation, decided with the user)

While Octopus drives a Wallbox charger over OCPP, the Wallbox cloud refuses pause and resume (HTTP 409), which Predbat's existing held_by_ocpp() guard already knows. So the Wallbox component gets:

  • (a) An OCPP connection toggle. A switch per charger that writes the charger's OCPP configuration (v3/chargers/<id>/ocpp-configuration, POST), changing type between ocpp and wallbox and keeping address, chargePointIdentity and password as they were read. The password is never logged.
  • (b) Force pause. Read and keep the OCPP configuration. Switch type to wallbox, wait until the charger reports it has left OCPP mode, and only then send the pause (remote-action 2). Read back and re-pause until it reports paused (status_id 182, 0 W).
  • (c) Force resume. Switch type back to ocpp only; no resume command is sent. Octopus restarts the charge itself once the link is back (connectionStatus 4). Retry the OCPP write on failure, including HTTP 502, and keep checking until the link reconnects.

Only a charger that was in OCPP mode when the hold began has its OCPP switched off and on. One that was already in Wallbox mode (e.g. Predbat-led) just gets a plain pause and resume.

Zappi force hold (second implementation)

The myenergi component already sets Zappi modes (set_mode(), with ZAPPI_MODE_STOPPED): - Force pause: keep the current mode (Eco+, Eco or Fast) in the hold state, then set Stopped. Stopped overrides the supplier's active command. - Force resume: set the kept mode back.

Leaving a Zappi in Stopped can confuse Octopus's scheduling, so the safety resume matters most here. This needs its own live test before it's trusted.

Ohme force hold (third implementation)

The Ohme component already has both calls: async_pause_charge() (POST /v1/chargeSessions/<serial>/stop, shown on a Home Pro as "Charging Paused") and async_resume_charge() (.../resume). - Force pause: pause the session. - Force resume: resume it.

Ohme's own guidance warns that a session left paused for a long time can make Octopus and Ohme raise a "failed session" or communication alert, because the car isn't taking power when told to. So, as with the Zappi, the hold is kept short and the safety resume matters most. Open questions for its live test: - Does Octopus's next instruction override the pause mid-slot? - Does resume hand the session back to Octopus's schedule, or start a charge regardless?

This also covers cars whose slots the Ohme component owns (car_slot_owner "ohme").

Hypervolt force hold

The Hypervolt component already stops and starts a charger: sync.apply {"release": false} over the charger's websocket stops it, and {"release": true} lets it charge again (release_command()). - Force pause: release false. - Force resume: release true.

There is no OCPP-style link to switch off: Octopus reaches a Hypervolt through Hypervolt's cloud. The existing code says it isn't known whether Octopus can undo a Hypervolt stop (charger_control_hand_to_octopus() in hypervolt.py). The verify step will show it: if Octopus overrides the pause, the charger reports charging again during the hold. Testers' feedback decides whether a Hypervolt needs the supplier suspend step below.

Other chargers

GivEnergy's charger follows when its force-pause mechanism is found and live-tested. Until then its cars keep the hard block.

Supplier suspend: not in the first version (decided with the user)

The first version never suspends the supplier's smart charging; the charger hold does the work alone. Holding at the charger and suspending the supplier was considered. The evidence so far: - For Wallbox it doesn't help. The 409 in the live test came while Octopus was suspended; switching OCPP off is what unlocked the charger, and Octopus stayed tidy without a suspend. - It might help chargers that Octopus drives cloud-to-cloud (Hypervolt, Ohme), if Octopus overrides their pause. It might also quiet the "failed session" alerts Ohme and myenergi warn about when a car ignores instructions.

Users with those chargers will test the first version and report back. If a charger turns out to need it, the suspend becomes a per-charger step in the hold, off by default, with this ordering: - Hold: pause the charger first, then suspend. A charger left without Octopus's control charges at full power, as the Pulsar did. - Release: unsuspend first, then release the charger, so it never charges at peak in the gap.

It would also bring back the rule that a temporary suspension is not a suspension. Predbat's Octopus component drops a suspended device from num_cars and rewires its slots within a minute, as seen in the live test, so a held device would have to stay active with its slots kept.

The updateDeviceSmartControl mutation (SUSPEND / UNSUSPEND) is confirmed from the live schema.

Live test, 2026-10-31 (user's Wallbox Pulsar on Octopus Intelligent, OCPP)

Octopus suspend: doesn't hold the car. updateDeviceSmartControl(input: {deviceId, action}), with action SUSPEND or UNSUSPEND (confirmed by schema introspection): - 18:56: suspended. The device went SMART_CONTROL_OFF, isSuspended true. - Octopus did not withdraw the planned dispatches. - 19:01: the Pulsar was charging at full power anyway. - Predbat's existing suspended-device handling dropped the car's Intelligent slots within a minute ("Live intelligent devices changed … to []") and fell back to its own car plan. - 19:03: unsuspended, and Octopus control returned straight away.

Wallbox pause under OCPP: refused. HTTP 409 "the charger cannot do that in its current state".

Wallbox with OCPP switched off: works. - 19:10:06: OCPP switched off (type wallbox). The write was accepted at once. - 19:10:21: first pause. Accepted, but the charger hadn't finished leaving OCPP mode and kept charging. - 19:12:02: second pause. The car stopped (status_id 182, 0 W). The Wallbox app and HA's Wallbox sensor kept showing "charging" for several minutes; the API's last_sync time is the trustworthy one. - Octopus kept the device SMART_CONTROL_IN_PROGRESS and kept its dispatches throughout. - 19:17:01: switching OCPP back on returned HTTP 502. The paused charger hadn't synced with the Wallbox cloud since 19:13:48. - 19:21:39: retried. Accepted, with connectionStatus 2. - 19:24:07: connectionStatus 4. Octopus restarted the charge at 7.06 kW without any resume command.

Build order (decided with the user)

  1. Wallbox force hold, in the Wallbox component: the OCPP toggle, force pause and force resume, with a way to call each by hand (the existing wallbox.py command line). Live re-test on the user's charger with the real code.
  2. The force-hold interface and shared hold rules in CarChargerControl.
  3. The planner work: switch, valuation, rates, kernel and publishing. The Predbat-led half depends on none of the above and can be built alongside.
  4. Zappi, Ohme and Hypervolt force holds. The user can't test these, so they ship marked as needing testers' feedback, which also decides whether any of them needs the supplier suspend step.

Constraints

  • Switch off ⇒ no behaviour change and no measurable optimiser slowdown.
  • Unit tests for all new code (repo rule), including:
  • Python/kernel parity for the cancelled-slot input.
  • The valuation: cancellation wins on a saving-session rate, loses on a normal export rate, and doesn't look free when the slot ends at the ready time.
  • The rate reversion cases: dispatch outside off-peak, inside off-peak, another car's live dispatch on the same minute, saving-session overlap, and the window billing rule.
  • The cancel set derived identically in trials, the final prediction, the old-plan re-score and a reused/restored plan.
  • Cars with neither Predbat-led slots nor a force-hold charger never cancelled.
  • Car-plan publishing.
  • The hold lifecycle: pause timing, the commitment rule and extension, verify-and-repeat on both pause and resume, restart mid-hold, a failed resume retried (including a 502), the safety resume, and the user's own OCPP-off or Stopped setting left alone.
  • Wallbox: OCPP write keeps address, identity and password and never logs the password; pause only after the mode change has landed.
  • Tests share one PredBat fixture: restore any state the new tests change.
  • Docs: document the switch in docs/ (car charging, Octopus Intelligent and the charger component pages), British English.

Left to the builder

  • The per-trial cancellation representation in Python and the kernel.
  • Exactly how the plan display marks a slot cancelled for export.
  • The safety-resume margin and retry cadence.
  • How each charger reports "stopped" and "handed back" for the verify step, within what's described above.
