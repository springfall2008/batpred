### Attribution of the clamp removal (brief Step 2)

At `pv_metric90_weight = 0` no pv90 prediction runs, so the only behavioural change reaching the
planner is `compute_metric`'s move from the pv10 downside-only clamp

```python
if metric10 > metric:
    metric += self.pv_metric10_weight * (metric10 - metric)
```

to the unconditional signed average. These differ only on candidates where `metric10 < metric`.
Instrumenting `compute_metric` shows that branch **is** exercised and is still plan-neutral:

| case | `compute_metric` calls | candidates with `metric10 < metric` | largest gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| `predbat_no_export_100.txt` | 387 | 0 | — |
| `cases/predbat_debug_agile1.yaml` | ~3 538 | 1 | 65.99 p |
| `cases/predbat_debug_pre_saving1.yaml` | ~6 082 | 9 | — |

Despite `agile1` and `pre_saving1` both containing candidates the old clamp would have suppressed,
both produce identical plans to the merge-base. **No debug case changed. Nothing needs regenerating
for Stage A.**

only `pv_forecast_minute` and `pv_forecast_minute10`. The guard in `calculate_plan` therefore fires
and copies p50. Measured after the run:

```

pv_total_kwh   = 16.24   pv90_total_kwh = 16.24
```

Same for `agile1` (2.295 / 2.295) and `pre_saving1` (2.427 / 2.427). Stage B is a **load-only**

### 2. Where the plan landed

It did not land anywhere — no movement, on either the target case or `agile1`. Per the brief's rubric

## Why the target case does not move: the metric surface

The charge limit of window 0 (23:30 → 05:30, the overnight charge in question) was swept from 30 % to
100 % and each scenario scored independently, with `end_record = 2300`, `soc_max = 9.52`,
`rate_min = 6.9`, `inverter_loss = 0.96`, `battery_loss = 0.97`.

The residual battery credit is `max(6.9/0.96/0.97, 1.0, rate_export_min) = 7.4098 p/kWh`
(`rate_export_min` is negative here because the export rate is 0). That is exactly the marginal cost
of storing a kWh bought at 6.9 p, which is what makes the mid metric flat.

Metric across the plateau (66 % → 100 %), in pence:

| scenario weighting | 66 % | 80 % | 100 % | spread (100 % − 66 %) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| nominal only (`w10=0, w90=0`) | 374.4728 | 374.4728 | 374.4728 | **0.000** — dead flat, as expected |
| pv10 only (`w10=1`) | — | — | — | **−62.973** — pulls hard to 100 % |
| **pv90 only (`w90=1`)** | 354.1198 | 354.1198 | 354.1198 | **0.000 — also dead flat** |
| legacy blend (`w10=0.1`) | 392.5841 | 389.9911 | 386.2869 | −6.297 |
| new blend (`w10=0.1, w90=0.1`) | 390.5488 | 387.9558 | 384.2516 | **−6.297 (identical)** |
| new blend (`w10=0.1, w90=0.5`) | 382.4077 | 379.8146 | 376.1104 | **−6.297 (identical)** |

**This is the finding.** With the p50 fallback, the pv90 metric is flat across the same plateau the
mid metric is flat across, for exactly the same reason (the residual credit equals the marginal
storage cost, and lower load does not change that). Since the blend is
`(1−w10−w90)·mid + w10·m10 + w90·m90`, and both `mid` and `m90` have zero slope, the blend's slope is
`w10 · slope10` regardless of `w90`. Raising w90 takes weight away from `mid` (flat) and gives it to
`m90` (also flat), leaving the pv10 pull completely untouched.

`export_kwh` is **0.0 at every charge limit in both the nominal and the pv90 scenario** — with the
same PV as p50 and 33.5 kWh of load against 16.2 kWh of PV, nothing ever spills, so there is no spill
for pv90 to price.

Consequence: **no value of `pv_metric90_weight` can move this plan.** Confirmed empirically by
scanning w90 = 0.0 … 1.0 with the real (fallback) p90 series — the plan is `[100 %, 100 %]` at all
nine weights tested.

## Would a real p90 PV series fix it? A synthetic probe

To separate "the mechanism is broken" from "the dump lacks the data", `pv_forecast_minute90` was
replaced with `p50 × factor` and the plan recomputed. This is **directional evidence only** — a real
Solcast p90 is peakier around midday rather than a uniform scale-up, and would clip harder for the
same daily total, so the numbers below are an approximation of the shape of the effect, not a
measurement of real p90 behaviour.

| p90 factor | p90 total kWh | pv90 spill at 100 % | pv90 metric spread (66 %→100 %) | pv10 spread | breakeven w90 at w10 = 0.1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.00 (the real fallback) | 16.24 | 0.000 kWh | **+0.000** | −62.973 | never |
| 1.15 | 18.67 | 0.418 kWh | +2.196 | −62.973 | 2.87 |
| 1.25 | 20.30 | 1.137 kWh | +6.502 | −62.973 | 0.97 |
| 1.50 | 24.36 | 2.928 kWh | +17.135 | −62.973 | 0.37 |
| 2.00 | 32.48 | 9.895 kWh | +19.364 | −62.973 | 0.33 |

With a real p90 the pv90 term does acquire the intended positive gradient: it penalises 100 % because
the battery starts spilling to a 0 p export. But the pv10 downside pull in this dump is 63 p across
the plateau, and a 1.5× p90 only musters 17 p against it.

Where the plan actually lands when w90 is raised:

| w90 | p90 = 1.0× (real fallback) | p90 = 1.5× (synthetic) | p90 = 2.0× (synthetic) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0 | 100 % | 100 % | 100 % |
| 0.1 | 100 % | 100 % | 100 % |
| 0.2 | 100 % | 100 % | 100 % |
| 0.3 | 100 % | 100 % | 100 % |
| 0.4 | 100 % | **71 %** | **66 %** |
| 0.5 | 100 % | **71 %** | **66 %** |
| 0.7 | 100 % | **68 %** | **63 %** |
| 1.0 | 100 % | **68 %** | **34 %** |

Two things worth noting. First, when the term does bite it lands at an **interior** value (71 %,
68 %) rather than collapsing to the plateau floor — so the gradient it produces is real, the strong
shape, not a tiebreak flip. Second, it only bites at w90 ≈ 0.4, four times the weight Task 9
proposes, and only when handed a p90 that is 50 % above p50.

## Verdict

**pv90 does not fix the scenario it was built for, at the proposed weight, on the available data.**

Precisely:

1. On `predbat_no_export_100.txt` the plan stays at `[100 %, 100 %]` at w90 = 0, 0.05, 0.1 and 0.2 —
   and at every weight up to 1.0. Per the brief's rubric this is "no movement", i.e. inconclusive on
   its face.
2. It is stronger than inconclusive, though, because the reason is identified and is not "we need
   more p90 data to see the effect": with the p50 fallback the pv90 metric is **provably flat** over
   the plateau (spread 0.000 p) and `export_kwh` is 0 in the pv90 scenario, so pv90's contribution to
   the blend's *slope* is exactly zero at any weight. The load-only lower bound is not "small", it is
   **identically zero**.
3. The mechanism is not broken — with a synthetic p90 carrying genuinely more PV, pv90 does price the
   spill and does produce an interior landing. But it needs w90 ≈ 0.4 against this dump's 63 p pv10
   pull, and even then only with a p90 ≥ 1.5× p50.
4. Neither `agile1` nor the target case changes at w90 = 0.1. `pre_saving1` does change from
   w90 = 0.05, partly through search-path perturbation rather than objective change alone.

So: **weak/negative, not strong.** The counterweight exists and is correctly wired, but at 0.1 it is
roughly 4× too light to overcome the pv10 hedge in the very case that motivated it, and against real
Solcast p90 data it would still be fighting a 63 p pull with (extrapolating) something in the
6–20 p range.

Honest caveat on what is *not* established: no dump in the repo carries a real `forecast90`, so the
1.15×–2.0× synthetic is the only evidence about non-zero p90 gradients, and its uniform-scale shape
understates the midday clipping a real p90 would cause. A dump with real Solcast p90 could show a
materially larger spread than the table above. It would have to be about 4× larger to change the
conclusion at w90 = 0.1.

## Recommendation on Task 9

**Do not flip the default from 0.0 to 0.1.** Reasons, in order of weight:

1. It buys nothing on the motivating case. At 0.1 the no-export plan is unchanged, so the default
   change would not deliver the behaviour the feature was requested for.
2. It costs ~18–28 % of plan time on every user's every cycle (measured above), because a third full
   prediction runs per candidate.
3. It is not inert on other cases: `pre_saving1` changes at w90 ≥ 0.05. So the default flip *would*
   require regenerating at least one debug-case expectation, for a change that does not fix the
   target case. (`agile1` is unaffected and would not need regenerating.)
4. Some of that `pre_saving1` movement is search-path perturbation rather than a cleaner objective,
   which makes the regenerated expectation hard to review honestly.

Suggested alternatives, in preference order:

- **Ship at default 0.0** and leave `pv_metric90_weight` as an expert opt-in. Tasks 1–7 are sound,
  regression-clean, and the plumbing is correct — that is worth landing on its own.
- **Get a dump with a real `forecast90`** before revisiting the default. That single artefact would
  replace the synthetic table above and is the only thing that can settle point 3 of the verdict.
- If the zero-export over-charging is to be fixed now, the evidence points at a different lever: the
  flat mid plateau comes from the residual credit being *exactly* the marginal storage cost, and the
  decision is then made entirely by a 63 p pv10 pull with no opposing term. Capping the residual
  credit by the realistic *usable* headroom, or damping the pv10 hedge when the export rate is 0,
  would both attack the actual cause more directly than adding a weight that has to out-shout it.
